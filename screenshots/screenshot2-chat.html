Attention Is All You Need: Revisited

Published: January 2024 · Machine Learning

We revisit the Transformer architecture and propose several improvements to the attention mechanism. Our experiments on standard benchmarks show consistent improvements over the baseline while maintaining computational efficiency.

The key contributions of this paper include: (1) a novel sparse attention pattern that reduces quadratic complexity to linear, (2) an improved positional encoding scheme that better captures long-range dependencies, and (3) a comprehensive evaluation across 8 NLP tasks.

Our results demonstrate that the proposed modifications achieve state-of-the-art performance on machine translation, text summarization, and question answering benchmarks while reducing training time by 40%.

What are the main contributions of this paper?
The paper makes three key contributions:
  • Sparse attention — reduces complexity from O(n²) to O(n) while maintaining quality [source: 1]
  • Improved positional encoding — better captures long-range dependencies in text [source: 1]
  • State-of-the-art results — achieves top performance on translation, summarization, and QA with 40% faster training [source: 2]
How does the sparse attention work?
The sparse attention pattern selectively attends to relevant tokens rather than computing full pairwise attention. This reduces the computational cost from quadratic to linear while preserving the model's ability to capture important relationships in the input.
